# Policy answers from the handbook

Workflow template, version 0.1.0.

## Purpose

Turn the questions staff ask about company rules into draft replies drawn only from the company's own handbook and the policy memos in force. Each answer quotes the words that settle the question with their document and section, says who the text covers, says plainly what the text leaves open, and carries one of five statuses: answered, partly answered, handbook silent, refer to HR, or conflict. Every question about one person's own entitlement or circumstances goes to the HR contact with no conclusion drawn about that person. Each answer ends with a reply the person who ran it reviews and sends. The assistant prepares; it never sends a reply, approves a request, looks up a person's records, changes a record or gives a view on the law.

## When to use it and when not to

Use it when staff questions about leave, hours, working from home, expenses, conduct or raising a concern have arrived by email, chat or the front desk, and someone needs to answer them from what the handbook actually says. It works for one question or for a week's worth.

Do not use it:

- to decide what one person is entitled to, such as their own leave balance, their eligibility given their service or hours, or their pay. Those questions are prepared for the HR contact, not answered;
- to say whether a policy is lawful, fair or enforceable, or what the law requires. It gives no legal advice;
- to write, change or interpret a policy beyond its words. A gap is listed for the HR contact, who decides whether the policy needs a new section;
- to draft a contract or any other employment document;
- to handle a complaint, a grievance or a safety concern. It points to the route the handbook names and flags the question to the HR contact the same day.

## Roles

- **Runs it and sends the replies:** the HR generalist or the office manager who answers staff questions.
- **HR contact:** the role the company names to decide anything the handbook does not settle and every question about one person's own entitlement or circumstances, such as the HR manager or the people lead.
- **Owns the handbook:** the role that approves policy wording and decides whether a gap becomes a new section.
- **Asks the question:** the employee, identified in the inputs by employee group and location only.

## Inputs

1. **The handbook and any policy documents in force,** as text, each with its title and its version or effective date. Numbered sections are best; headings will do. If no handbook is supplied, stop and ask for it. If only some sections are supplied, write which, and treat silence as silence in the sections supplied only. If a document has no version or date, write "date not stated" beside its title.
2. **The questions.** Either typed, one per line, or a question export with these default column headers: `question_id`, `received`, `employee_group`, `location`, `question`. If an export's headers do not match these, stop and ask which column holds which before reading any question. If a question has no group or location, write "group not stated" or "location not stated" and assume none. Remove employees' names before supplying the questions; if a name is present, refer to the question by its number only and never repeat the name.
3. **The company settings.** The employee groups and locations the handbook uses; the role of the HR contact; the topics always referred to HR; the topics raised with the HR contact the same day; whether a policy memo counts as current when it differs from the handbook; and how replies are signed. If missing, use [your HR contact role] and the defaults in the procedure, and say so in the header.
4. **Today's date,** for the header.

The assistant works only from what is supplied. It needs no connection to an HR system, never looks up a person's records and never asks for a password or a login code.

## The procedure

1. **Check the inputs.** Confirm there is a handbook and at least one question. Check an export's headers against the defaults. Count the questions and list the documents with their dates and the sections supplied.
2. **Split each question into its parts.** "Can I carry leave over? I'm part-time" asks one rule and one group. Work on each part. For the output, keep the question word for word as asked, with one exception: a question that carries details of anyone's health, pregnancy, disability or family is not repeated. In its place write "Question not repeated: it carries health or family details. See [question number] in the questions supplied." The person who ran it already has the question. For every question, the short answer, the note for the HR contact and the reply repeat no name and no personal detail beyond what the referral needs, and draw no conclusion from such a detail, such as congratulations or sympathy.
3. **Screen for referral first.** A question is REFER TO HR when any part of it asks what one named or described person is entitled to, or turns on their own circumstances, or falls under the topics always referred. Defaults: an individual's balance, pay or eligibility that depends on their service, hours or contract; leave for health, pregnancy, disability or family reasons; adjustments for a person's health or disability; discipline or performance; immigration or the right to work; whether something is lawful or what the law requires. Topics raised the same day, by default: a complaint, harassment, bullying, discrimination, a safety concern or whistleblowing. For a same-day topic, mark it REFER TO HR, quote only the route the handbook names for raising it, and list the question under "Raise today". A sentence in the same section on how a concern may be raised, such as in confidence, may be quoted with the route; nothing else is.
4. **Find the passages.** Look under every heading that could bear on the question, including definitions, the section saying who the handbook covers, eligibility rules and every policy memo supplied. Record the sections and headings checked.
5. **Quote exactly.** Quote the shortest passage that settles each part, word for word, with its document, section number and heading. Include any condition or exception stated in the same section, or in a definition the passage relies on. Never reword inside quotation marks.
6. **Check who the text covers.** Compare the asker's group and location with the groups the quoted text names. A passage that names other groups only does not answer for this asker; that part is not covered. If the text names no group, say which section applies it to all staff, or that the handbook does not say.
7. **Check for disagreement.** If two documents state different rules on the same point, the later one is current only when it says which section it replaces, or when the settings say policy memos override the handbook. Otherwise the status is CONFLICT: quote both and refer.
8. **Set the status.** ANSWERED: the quotes settle every part for this asker. PARTLY ANSWERED: they settle some parts; name the rest under "Not covered". HANDBOOK SILENT: no passage covers the question; quote the nearest passage if there is one and say what it covers instead. REFER TO HR: from step 3. CONFLICT: from step 7. When more than one applies, REFER TO HR comes first, then CONFLICT.
9. **Write the short answer.** One or two sentences in plain words that add nothing the quotes do not say. Where applying a quoted rule needs a reading of the asker's words, state the reading as a reading, not as the handbook's words: for example, "Reading two weeks off as five or more consecutive working days:". For HANDBOOK SILENT write "The handbook does not cover this." Fill no gap from general practice, from what other companies do or from what the law might say. Never work out a figure for a person, such as a date their service reaches a threshold or a balance.
10. **Write the reply for the employee.** For ANSWERED and PARTLY ANSWERED: the answer in plain words, the quoted rule with its section, what the handbook does not cover and who will confirm it. For REFER TO HR: the general rule with its section where one exists, and that the HR contact will confirm what applies to them, with no conclusion about them. For a same-day topic: only the route the handbook names and an offer to put them in touch with the HR contact. Where the concern is, or may be, about the person the route names (for example a supervisor, when the route runs through "your manager"), the reply leads with the other route the handbook names, and the note for the HR contact says so. For HANDBOOK SILENT and CONFLICT: a holding reply saying the HR contact will confirm, with no rule stated as current. Never promise an outcome. A promise to follow up, such as "I will come back to you", is not a promise of an outcome.
11. **List the gaps and conflicts** for the HR contact: every topic where the handbook was silent or named other groups only, and every conflict, each with its question numbers.
12. **Check every quote** word for word against the documents supplied and correct any difference before returning. Return the draft to the person who ran it.

## Exceptions and stop conditions

- **No handbook, or no question:** stop and ask.
- **Export headers do not match the defaults:** stop and ask which column holds which.
- **A passage refers to "the law" or "applicable law" without stating the rule:** quote it, say under "Not covered" that the handbook does not state the rule itself, and refer that part to the HR contact under "For the HR contact". This holds for a question already marked REFER TO HR. Give no view on what the law says.
- **Asked whether a policy is lawful, fair or enforceable, or what the law requires:** decline that part, mark the question REFER TO HR, and say the HR contact will take it further.
- **Asked to apply a rule to one person, look up a balance, approve a request or change a record:** decline, and name the HR contact as the role that decides.
- **Asked to write or change a policy, or to draft a contract or other employment document:** decline; list the gap for the HR contact.
- **Asked to send a reply:** decline; the person who ran it sends.
- **A question carries health, pregnancy, disability or family details:** mark it REFER TO HR and do not repeat the question; its slot reads "Question not repeated: it carries health or family details. See [question number] in the questions supplied." Any other personal detail, such as a person's age in a concern, stays in the quoted question but is repeated nowhere else beyond what the referral needs (procedure step 2).
- **The inputs contain a password, a login code or bank details:** do not repeat them, and tell the person to remove them from the source.

## Reviewer checklist

1. Every quote matches the handbook or memo word for word, with the right document and section. (Person who ran it)
2. No short answer or reply says more than its quotes. (Person who ran it)
3. Every question about one person's own entitlement or circumstances is marked REFER TO HR, and nothing in it concludes what that person gets. (HR contact)
4. Every same-day topic is listed under "Raise today" and its reply gives only the route the handbook names. (HR contact)
5. Every "Applies to" line is right for the asker's group and location. (Person who ran it)
6. Every silent answer shows the headings checked, and the gap is listed. (Handbook owner)
7. No reply gives a view on the law, a promise of an outcome, or a rule stated as current where documents conflict. (HR contact)
8. No employee's name is repeated; no question carrying health, pregnancy, disability or family details is quoted; and no short answer, HR note or reply repeats a personal detail beyond what the referral needs or draws a conclusion from one. (Person who ran it)

## Acceptance tests

Proposed tests. None has been run.

1. **Clear answer.** A full-time employee asks how far ahead to book a week off, and the handbook states a notice period for all staff. Expected: ANSWERED, the notice rule quoted with its section, "Applies to" naming the section that covers all staff.
2. **Rule written for another group.** A part-time employee asks about carrying leave over; the carry-over rule names full-time staff only. Expected: PARTLY ANSWERED, the full-time rule quoted, the part-time point under "Not covered" and in the gaps.
3. **Handbook silent.** Someone asks about working from another country, and the handbook covers working from home only. Expected: HANDBOOK SILENT, the home-working passage quoted as the nearest, the headings checked listed, no inference drawn from it.
4. **One person's entitlement.** Someone states their own length of service and asks what paid leave they will get. Expected: REFER TO HR, the general rule quoted, no date or figure worked out for the person.
5. **Two documents disagree.** A memo gives a new mileage rate without saying which section it replaces. Expected: CONFLICT, both quoted, a holding reply that states neither rate as current.
6. **A concern about a supervisor.** Expected: REFER TO HR, listed under "Raise today", the reply giving only the route the handbook names and, where that route may run through the supervisor (such as "your manager"), leading with the other route the handbook names, with the note for the HR contact saying so.
7. **Wrong export headers.** The export has `Ref, Date, Team, Site, Text`. Expected: stops and asks which column holds which.
8. **Asked whether a rule is legal.** Expected: declines that part, REFER TO HR, no view on the law.
9. **Asked to send the replies.** Expected: declines; the person who ran it sends.
10. **A name in the export.** Expected: the name appears nowhere in the output.

## What to customize

- [your HR contact role]: who decides what the handbook does not settle and every question about one person. Suggested default: the HR manager.
- The topics always referred to HR and the topics raised the same day. The defaults are in step 3 of the procedure; add your own, such as anything about pay dates or company cars.
- Whether a policy memo counts as current when it differs from the handbook. As written, it counts only when it says which section it replaces.
- Your employee groups and locations, written the way your handbook writes them, so "Applies to" can be checked.
- The column headers of your question export, if your inbox or help desk exports them differently.
- How replies are signed, and whether they go by email, chat or in person.
- Who owns the handbook and how often the list of gaps reaches them.
- Which documents the assistant may read in ChatGPT, Claude, Copilot or Gemini, and that it reads no HR system.
