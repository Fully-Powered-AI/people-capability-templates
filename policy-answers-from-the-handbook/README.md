# Policy answers from the handbook

Staff questions about leave, hours, expenses and conduct get answers that quote your own handbook, name the section, and say plainly where the handbook is silent. Anything about one person's own entitlement goes to HR, and no reply leaves until a person has read it.

*Capability template · People and HR · version 0.1.0 · [Capabilities Directory](https://fullypowered.ai/capabilities/policy-answers-from-the-handbook/)*

## The outcome

Every policy question gets the same careful answer, whoever is on the desk that day. The reply quotes the handbook's own words with the section beside them, says who the rule covers, and says just as plainly what the handbook does not cover. Nobody is told a rule from memory, and nobody is told a rule that was written for a different group of staff.

The questions that should never be answered at the front desk go where they belong. Anything about what one person is entitled to, anything touching health, family or a person's own circumstances, and anything asking what the law requires goes to your HR contact, with the general rule quoted and no conclusion drawn about the person. A complaint or a safety concern gets the route your handbook names and is flagged to the HR contact the same day.

Over time the answers show where the handbook is thin. Every silence and every clash between two documents is listed for the person who owns the handbook.

## Who starts the work, and when

The HR generalist or the office manager who answers staff questions runs it whenever questions come in: one at a time as they arrive, or a batch at the end of the week. That person reads every answer, edits the replies and sends them. The HR contact picks up every question marked for them.

## What they supply

- **The handbook,** as text, with its version or effective date, and any policy memos in force.
- **The questions,** typed or exported from the inbox or help desk, with each asker's employee group and location and no names.
- **The company settings:** the employee groups your handbook uses, who your HR contact is, which topics always go to HR, and whether a memo overrides the handbook.

## What comes out

One page of answers. Each question carries a status (answered, partly answered, handbook silent, refer to HR, or conflict), a short answer, the exact words of the handbook with their section, who the text covers, what it leaves open, the headings that were checked, what the HR contact needs to decide, and a reply ready to review.

At the end: the gaps and conflicts for the handbook owner, and a line confirming every quote was checked against the documents supplied. Nothing is sent, approved or changed.

## What has to change for your company

Name your HR contact and agree which topics always go to them; the template starts from a careful default list, and many companies add their own. Decide whether a policy memo overrides the handbook or only counts when it says which section it replaces. Write down your employee groups and locations the way your handbook writes them. Agree who sends the replies and how they are signed, and who owns the list of gaps. It runs in ChatGPT, Claude, Copilot or Gemini from the documents you paste in; it needs no connection to your HR system and never looks up anyone's records.

## Start using it

1. Read [EXAMPLE.md](EXAMPLE.md) to see what a good result looks like.
2. Open [TEMPLATE.md](TEMPLATE.md) and set every item under "What to customize" to your own rules, roles and limits.
3. Give your AI assistant the instruction block and the output format from the template, with your own limits in place of the ones the instructions leave open in square brackets. Paste them in as custom instructions in ChatGPT, Claude, Copilot or Gemini, or copy this whole folder into a product that loads `SKILL.md` packages. This folder stands alone; you do not need the rest of the repository.
