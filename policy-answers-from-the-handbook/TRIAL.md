# Policy answers from the handbook: trial on the invented example

- **Product:** Claude, run in Claude Code
- **Model:** Claude Sonnet 5.5 (model ID claude-sonnet-5-5)
- **Date:** 2026-11-09

The inputs were the invented example in [example.md](example.md): its company settings, handbook, policy memo and six-question export. No client data was used. The run was made by a model that did not write the template. It read the complete template and only the inputs of the example, followed the instruction block and output format exactly, and read the example's own output only afterwards.

## What came out

All six questions were answered in the output format, with every required part present. The statuses were: Q1 answered, Q2 partly answered, Q3 handbook silent, Q4 referred to HR, Q5 conflict, Q6 referred to HR and listed under "Raise today". The header counted 1, 1, 1, 2, 1. Gaps listed: part-time carry-over (Q2) and working from another address or country (Q3). One conflict: Q5, handbook section 6.2 against the finance memo. Every quote was checked against the supplied text and matched. The run decided no request, worked out no figure for anyone, gave no view on the law and sent nothing.

The substance of each answer:

- **Q1.** Quoted sections 3.3 (four weeks' notice for five or more consecutive working days) and 3.4 (July and August requests in the order received from 1 March). Section 1.1 applies it to all staff; the depot condition in 3.4 does not cover a head-office asker.
- **Q2.** Quoted section 3.5, which names full-time staff only, and section 3.2. Whether a part-time person may carry leave over was put under "Not covered" and in the gaps. The reply says the HR manager will confirm.
- **Q3.** Nothing found. The nearest passages were sections 5.1 and 5.2, which cover working from the address held in the HR system. Headings looked under: 1, 2, 5, 6. Gap listed, holding reply written.
- **Q4.** Marked REFER TO HR. The general rule in 4.1 and the notice rule in 4.3 were quoted. Section 4.2, which leaves further unpaid leave to "the law" without stating the rule, was quoted and passed to the HR contact with no view given. No date, balance or conclusion was drawn for the asker. The question text was not repeated in the output, because it carries family details (see the second observation below).
- **Q5.** CONFLICT. Quoted 6.2 (60 cents) and the memo (67 cents from 1 October). The memo names no section it replaces, and the settings say a memo is current only when it does, so the holding reply states neither rate as current.
- **Q6.** REFER TO HR and Raise today. The reply quotes only the route in section 7.1 (the manager or, if preferred, the HR manager) and offers to put the person in touch with the HR manager.

## What was observed

The template was followed without needing to stop. Nothing in the inputs triggered a stop condition. The points below are where the instructions were ambiguous or the run had to choose.

1. **Q1 and "two weeks".** Section 3.3 applies to "five or more consecutive working days". The asker wrote "two weeks", which the run read as meeting that threshold. The handbook does not define a week as five working days for leave, so this is a small inference. Neither the template nor the example says how to treat it. Section 3.4's "from 1 March" does not say which year's March for a request about next August, and the run left it unstated.
2. **Quoting the question against protecting personal details.** Step 2 says to keep each question word for word, and the output format has a slot for it. The stop conditions say that for a question with health or family details, repeat none of the details beyond what the referral needs. For Q4 the run followed the stop condition and left the question text out, with a pointer to the export. Q6 carries a personal characteristic (age) but no health or family detail, so the run kept its words. The template does not say whether age counts. The published example repeats Q4 in full, including the length of service and the family detail, and its reply addresses the person with "congratulations", a conclusion drawn from a detail the template says not to repeat.
3. **Q6 and section 7.2.** "Quote only the route the handbook names" leaves open whether "You can raise a concern in confidence" is part of the route. The run left it out of the quotes and the reply. The published example includes it. The route in 7.1 is "your manager or, if you prefer, directly with the HR manager", and the handbook's section 1.2 defines manager as the person you report to day to day, which may be the supervisor in the question. The template does not say what to do when the named route may run through the person complained of. The run quoted what was there and added nothing.
4. **Q5 as conflict.** The memo says journeys before 1 October use the old rate, which strongly implies it replaces section 6.2. The template's rule is strict: the memo must name the section or the settings must say memos override. The run applied the rule and marked CONFLICT. This is correct under the template, and it may send an obvious case to HR.
5. **Q4 and the "law" stop condition.** The example's inputs include a passage (4.2) that refers to the law without stating the rule. The run nearly missed that this needed handling under "Not covered" for a REFER TO HR question, because the status line and the stop condition sit in different parts of the template. The format does not say where the "refer that part" note goes for a referred question.
6. **Replies that say "I will come back to you".** The template says never promise an outcome. A promise to reply is not an outcome, but the line is not defined, and the run used the wording in two replies. A reviewer may want to cut it.
7. **Quote slots with two passages.** For Q3 the nearest passage is two sections (5.1 and 5.2). The format shows one nearest passage; the run gave two lines under it.
8. **Small slots.** "Applies to" for the memo, which has no numbered sections, had no empty wording to use; the run wrote that the memo names no group. "Settings not supplied" was left out because every setting was supplied.

**Differences from the published example.** The statuses, the gaps, the conflict, the "Raise today" entry and the counts are the same. The differences are in wording and in the three choices above: Q4's question text is withheld here and repeated there; Q6's reply here quotes the route only; the Q1 short answer here follows the quotes more closely. Every quote in both outputs matches the inputs word for word.

**Facts in either output that are not in the inputs.** None in this run beyond the "two weeks" reading in point 1. In the published example: "Hi, and congratulations" (Q4) assumes the nature of the event beyond the rule; the Q4 short answer, that applicability turns on "their service", is drawn from the quoted rule and not from the asker's own figure; the Q1 short answer's "at least four weeks" depends on the same two-week reading.

## Edits made after this run

The template and the published example were revised on 9 November 2026, after this run and after an independent review. These edits have not been run again. The recorded output and observations above are unchanged; where they compare this run with the published example, they describe the example as it stood at the time of the run. Each edit is listed with whether the output recorded above still fits it.

- **Quoting a question with personal details (observation 2).** The template now gives one rule in the procedure, the stop conditions, the pasted instructions and the output format: a question that carries details of anyone's health, pregnancy, disability or family is not repeated, and its slot reads "Question not repeated: it carries health or family details. See [question number] in the questions supplied." Every other question is quoted word for word. Any other personal detail, such as age in Q6, stays in the quoted question but is repeated nowhere else beyond what the referral needs, and no short answer, note or reply draws a conclusion from such a detail. This settles the point the way this run chose for Q4 and Q6. The published example was changed to match: its Q4 no longer repeats the question, and its reply no longer opens "Hi, and congratulations".
- **Raising a concern (observation 3).** A sentence in the same section on how a concern may be raised, such as "in confidence", may now be quoted with the route; it is allowed, not required, so this run's choice to leave it out still fits. New: where the concern is, or may be, about the person the route names, the reply leads with the other route the handbook names. This run's Q6 reply named the manager first, so under the revised instructions it would lead with the HR manager instead. The published example's Q6 reply was changed to lead with the HR manager.
- **Stated readings (observation 1).** Where applying a rule needs a reading of the asker's words, the short answer now states it as a reading, for example "Reading two weeks off as five or more consecutive working days:". This run made the reading without stating it, so its Q1 short answer would now carry that line. The published example's Q1 now states it. Which year's 1 March section 3.4 means is still left unstated.
- **The "law" note for a referred question (observation 5).** The template and output format now say the note goes under "Not covered", with the referral under "For the HR contact", also for a question already marked REFER TO HR. This run and the published example did this.
- **"I will come back to you" (observation 6).** The template now says a promise to follow up is not a promise of an outcome. This run's wording fits.
- **Two nearest passages and a document that names no group (observations 7 and 8).** The output format now allows two or more nearest passages, each with its section, in the same line, and gives "[document] names no group." for a document with no section on who it covers. This run's choices fit both.
- **Smaller corrections to the published example.** The memo is listed as issued 1 September 2026 and effective 1 October 2026; Q4's headings checked no longer include section 3 (Annual leave); Q5's include section 1, which its "Applies to" line cites.
- **Second round, 9 November 2026, after the independent review's minor points.** The pasted instructions now carry the input rules already in the Inputs section: a handbook supplied in part counts as silent only in the sections supplied, and a missing date, group, location or received date takes the "not stated" wording in the output format. The procedure now says outright that a same-day topic is marked REFER TO HR, and acceptance test 6 now expects the reply to lead with the other route. In the published example, the Q1 reply now states the two-week reading as a reading, and the Q6 reply leads with the HR manager and says the handbook also names the manager as a route. None of this was run again; the example's inputs carry no partial handbook and no missing date, group or location, so the output recorded above is unaffected by the instruction change.
- **Version notes and the public profile** now say the template was tried on its invented example in Claude on 9 November 2026, with this record.

**What this does not show.** It was one run, by one model, on an invented example. It was not tested in a client environment or in other products, and it says nothing about how the template behaves on a real handbook.
