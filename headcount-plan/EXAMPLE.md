# Headcount plan: an invented example

*Illustrative example with invented data.* The company, the people, the roles, the pay, the budget, the revenue and the dates below are all invented. Company A is an invented 16-person software company preparing its plan for 2027 before the board's budget meeting in December. Its finance and people lead prepares the plan for the chief executive. First come the inputs the finance and people lead supplied, in full, then the plan the capability returned.

## How the figures were worked

So that every figure can be checked against the inputs:

- An employee row costs (pay ÷ 12) × (1 + bonus target) × 1.20 + \$500 a person each month. A software engineer at today's pay: \$140,000 ÷ 12 × 1.20 + \$500 = \$14,500, so six cost \$87,000. The account executives: \$200,000 ÷ 12 × 1.20 × 1.20 + \$1,000 = \$25,000.
- From April the current roles rise 4%: together \$213,092 a month, against \$205,300 before.
- P2 takes away one customer success manager at the role's average of \$90,000: \$9,500 a month in February and March and \$9,860 from April, \$107,740 over the year.
- P1 costs \$29,000 a month for two engineers: ten months from March is \$290,000, plus \$6,000 one-off. In the lower case, seven months from June is \$203,000, plus \$6,000.
- April, base case: \$213,092 − \$9,860 (P2) + \$29,000 (P1) + \$9,700 (P3) + \$14,900 (P4) + \$3,000 (P4's one-off) = \$259,832.
- The base year is the current roles (\$615,900 for January to March and \$1,917,828 for April to December) plus the changes (\$296,000 − \$107,740 + \$100,000 + \$137,100 + \$96,000): \$2,533,728 + \$521,360 = \$3,055,088.
- The lower case leaves out P4 and P5 and moves P1 to June; June carries P1's one-off cost, which is why it alone goes over. The higher case adds P6's \$61,000.
- The check: \$205,300 − \$201,800 = \$3,500, which is 1.7% of \$201,800.
