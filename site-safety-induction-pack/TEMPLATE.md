# Site safety induction pack

Workflow template, version 0.1.0.

## Purpose

Prepare the safety induction for one named site, for the new starters and visiting contractors who will work there: the content, built only from that site's own emergency arrangements, rules and hazards; a short check of understanding with its answer key; a sign-off record for each person; and the refresher dates for each role. A competent person approves the pack and delivers the induction. The assistant prepares and checks. It never delivers the induction, marks anyone as inducted, signs a record, lets anyone onto the site, sends the pack, changes a training record or adds a control the site has not recorded.

## When to use it and when not to

Use it when people are about to start work at a site and need inducting: a new hire, a temporary or agency worker, or a contractor's crew arriving for a job. Run it again when the site's emergency arrangements, rules or hazards change, and when a refresher falls due.

Do not use it:

- for several sites at once. "All our depots" gets one pack per site, each from that site's documents;
- to find the hazards of a task or a place. That is the workplace risk assessment workflow template, whose findings this pack takes as input;
- to plan a new colleague's first ninety days in the role. That is the employee onboarding plan workflow template;
- to decide whether a contractor is safe to bring in. That is the contractor safety prequalification workflow template; the induction comes after that decision;
- for a visitor briefing for people who will not work on site;
- to say what the law requires of your company for inductions or training. That question goes to [your adviser].

## Roles

- **Runs it:** the site, facilities or people lead responsible for the people starting, such as a depot manager, a facilities manager or an HR administrator.
- **Approves the pack, delivers the induction and judges each person's check:** [your competent person], the person the company names as competent to induct people at this site, such as its health and safety coordinator.
- **Keeps the signed records and owns the refresher dates:** the role the brief names to keep the records, unless the brief names a different role to own the refresher dates.
- **Answers questions of law:** [your adviser].

## Inputs

1. **The induction brief.** The site's name and the areas the pack covers; the groups being inducted (new starters, which includes permanent, temporary and agency workers, and contractors) with the roles in each; for each contractor, the company, the work, the dates and where its safety prequalification stands; the competent person; how the induction is delivered (in person, on screen followed by a walk-round, or both); the date the pack is prepared; the role that keeps the records, and the role that owns the refresher dates if it is a different one. If the site is not named, or the brief covers more than one site, stop and ask which site. If no group or role is named, stop and ask. If the competent person is not named, write "competent person not named" and make it the first point for the competent person.
2. **The site's arrangements,** as documents or typed summaries, each with its date: the emergency plan; first aid and welfare; the site rules; the permit arrangements, when contractors are in the pack; how hazards, near misses and injuries are reported. Code them D1, D2 in the order supplied. If the emergency arrangements are not supplied, stop and ask for them.
3. **The hazard list,** from the site's risk assessments, one row per control, with these column headers: `hazard,area,who,control,control_level,source,source_date`. `who` holds roles separated by semicolons, or "All". `control_level` is one of Remove, Substitute, Engineering, Rules and procedures, Protective equipment. Dates are written YYYY-MM-DD. If a header does not match, stop and ask which column holds which field; never guess. If a `control_level` is not one of the five, stop and ask. If no hazard with at least one control is supplied, stop and ask.
4. **The people list,** one row per person, with these column headers: `person,group,role,employer,induction_date`. If a header does not match, stop and ask. If there is no people list, print one blank sign-off row for each role, to be filled in at the induction.
5. **The settings.** If any is missing, use its default and say so in the header:
   - **Check method for each role.** One of: questions; questions and walk-round; questions, walk-round and demonstration. Default: questions and walk-round.
   - **Demonstrations for each role** whose method includes one: the tasks the person shows they can do, such as opening a door from inside with its release. No default; a method with a demonstration and no tasks listed becomes a point for the competent person.
   - **Pass rule.** Default: every question answered correctly, every walk-round place shown and every demonstration done. A wrong answer is explained again and asked again once.
   - **Refresher interval for each role.** Default: 12 months. For contractors: at the start of each new job at the site or after 12 months, whichever comes first.
   - **Return after absence.** Default: a refresher after [6] months away from the site.
   - **Hazard source age.** Default: a hazard whose source is more than 12 months older than the pack date becomes a point for the competent person.

The assistant works only from what the person supplies. It needs no access to a training system, a door-entry system or any register, and never asks for a password or a login code.

## The procedure

1. **Check the brief.** Confirm one site, at least one group with its roles, and the competent person. Stop or record as set out under Inputs.
2. **Check the documents and the hazard list.** List the documents with their codes and dates. Check the headers of the hazard list and the people list. Stop as set out under Inputs.
3. **Take out personal details.** Names appear only in Part 4, the sign-off record. Health details, injuries and anything about a person's medical history appear nowhere in the pack, Part 4 included; nor do home addresses or phone numbers. The header names only the kinds of personal detail found and where they were left out, such as "names, kept to Part 4; one health detail, left out". When a health detail was found, give the person who ran it a short message after the pack and outside it: the notes held a health detail, it was left out of the pack, and they should raise it directly with the people team and the competent person. Neither the header nor the message repeats the detail, names or points to the person it concerns, or repeats any request tied to it, such as keeping someone away from an area.
4. **Match the hazards to the roles.** Number each hazard H1, H2 in the order it first appears in the list, grouping its rows. A hazard applies to a role when its `who` names that role or says "All". List any hazard that applies to no role in the pack under "Not in this pack". If a role in the brief has no hazard that applies to it, make it a point.
5. **Write the five topics.** Every line names the site's own arrangement and the document it came from; a line that draws on the hazard list cites the hazard's H code instead. Each topic must cover:
   - **Emergencies:** each alarm sound and what it means; the way out; the assembly point by name; who takes the roll; what to do on finding a fire or someone hurt.
   - **First aid and welfare:** how to reach a first aider; where the first aid room or kit is; toilets, break area and drinking water; who to tell when unwell.
   - **Site rules:** signing in and out; areas closed or needing permission; which protective equipment is worn where; walking routes; vehicle routes, meaning where forklifts or other vehicles run (a line of its own, even when the walking routes are given); smoking, and phones where the rules mention them.
   - **Site hazards:** each hazard in the pack with its area, the roles it applies to, and its controls in order, strongest first: Remove, Substitute, Engineering, Rules and procedures, Protective equipment. Controls at the same level keep the order of the list. Name the source and its date.
   - **Reporting:** what to report, to whom, and how it is recorded. Include what the documents say about raising a concern, when they say it.
   When a required item is not in the documents, write "MISSING: [item]" in its place and make it a point. Never fill it from general practice or another site.
6. **Mark weak or old hazards.** When every control for a hazard is at Rules and procedures or Protective equipment, write "Only rules and protective equipment recorded" under it and make it a point. Never add a control. When a hazard's source is older than the hazard source age, make it a point naming the source and its date.
7. **Write the contractor part.** When the pack has a contractor group: the company, its work and dates; which work needs a permit at this site, who issues it and when it is returned; the site rules that apply to contractors only; and where the safety prequalification stands, as the brief gives it. When the prequalification is not approved, is conditional with conditions still open, or is not stated, make it a point: the induction does not replace that decision. When there is no contractor group, write "No contractor group in this pack."
8. **Write the check of understanding.** One question on each of Emergencies, First aid and welfare, Site rules and Reporting; one on each hazard in the pack; and, when there is a contractor group, one on permits. Each question asks for one fact about this site (where, who, what, when), or at most two linked facts about the same arrangement, can be answered from the pack alone, and is never a yes-or-no or a general safety question. Mark who answers it: "All" for the four topics; the roles of the hazard for a hazard question; the contractor roles for the permits question. Give the answer key, citing the hazard or document. Then, for each role, the walk-round places: the assembly point, the nearest fire exit and alarm point to where the person will work, the first aid room or kit, and any emergency equipment the documents or controls place in an area of a hazard that applies to the role. Then the demonstrations from the settings, or "none for this role". End with the pass rule.
9. **Write the sign-off record.** One entry per person on the people list, in its order: name, group, role, employer, induction date, check method, the questions they answer, and the refresher date. Leave every result, outcome and signature blank: print the options for the competent person to mark. Where a role's check method leaves out the walk-round or the demonstration, print "not part of this role's check" on that line in place of the options; it comes from the settings and is not a result. With no people list, print one blank entry per role. The record shows that each part of the check was passed, not merely that the person was present, so it carries a result for each part.
10. **Set the refresher dates.** For each role: the interval, the next due date counted from the induction date, the triggers, and the owner: the role that keeps the records, unless the brief names a different owner ("owner not set" if the brief names neither, and a point). The triggers for every role: a change to the emergency arrangements, the site rules or a hazard in the pack that applies to the role; an incident or near miss involving the role or its area; a return after the absence in the settings.
11. **List the points for the competent person.** Each with the facts and a question, in this order: competent person not named; MISSING items; old hazard sources; hazards with only rules and protective equipment; roles with no hazard; contractor prequalification not settled; demonstration tasks not listed; refresher owner not set; documents that disagree with each other, quoting both. Recommend nothing.
12. **Return the draft** to the person who ran it, for the competent person to approve. Leave the approval block blank. Write every date in the pack as day, month name and year, such as 16 November 2026. When a health detail was found, the message from step 3 follows the pack, outside it.

## Exceptions and stop conditions

- **No named site, or more than one site:** stop and ask. One pack per site.
- **No emergency arrangements, or no hazard with a control:** stop and ask. Never write an induction from general knowledge.
- **Hazard list or people list headers that do not match, or an unknown control level:** stop and ask which column or level is meant.
- **Two documents that disagree,** such as two different assembly points: print both with their codes in the topic and make it a point. Do not choose.
- **Asked for one induction to use across several sites, or for no site in particular:** decline, and ask which site and for its documents.
- **Asked to treat attendance or a signature as enough, to drop the check, or to give a role a lighter check method than the settings set:** decline. A change to the settings is the competent person's to make, outside the pack.
- **Asked to mark a person as inducted, fill in a result, sign, let a person start work or onto the site, send the pack or update a training record:** decline and name the role that does it.
- **Asked to add a control, a hazard or an emergency arrangement the documents do not give:** decline. Write the question as a point.
- **Asked what the law requires for inductions or training:** decline. Write the question for [your adviser]; the pack cites no law or standard.
- **Notes or lists that hold a health detail, a home address or a phone number:** leave them out, as in step 3. The header names only the kind of detail, such as "one health detail, left out". The message about a health detail comes after the pack and outside it. Neither repeats the detail, the person or any request tied to it.
- **Documents that contain door codes, alarm codes, a password or a login code:** do not repeat them; tell the person to remove them from what is shared.

## Reviewer checklist

1. Every line of Part 1 names this site's own arrangement and a document or hazard code, and nothing comes from general practice. (Competent person)
2. Every hazard in the list appears once, with the roles it applies to, and its controls run strongest first with none added. (Competent person)
3. Each MISSING item, old source and hazard with only rules and protective equipment has a point. (Competent person)
4. Every question can be answered from the pack alone, and the answer key matches the documents. (Competent person)
5. The walk-round places exist where the pack says they are. (Person who ran it)
6. Names appear only in Part 4; no health detail appears anywhere, and neither the header nor the message after the pack names the person or repeats a request tied to the detail. (Person who ran it)
7. Every refresher date is the induction date plus the role's interval, and the owner is named or flagged. (Records keeper)
8. Contractors are inducted only once their prequalification is decided, or the point says it is not. (Competent person)
9. The results, signatures and approval block were left blank by the assistant. (Competent person)

## Acceptance tests

Proposed tests. None has been run.

1. **One pack for every site.** "We need one induction that works for all four of our warehouses." Expected: declines; asks which site comes first and for its documents.
2. **No emergency arrangements.** A hazard list and site rules, but no emergency plan. Expected: stops and asks for the emergency arrangements; no pack.
3. **Missing item.** An emergency plan that names the assembly point but not who takes the roll. Expected: "MISSING: who takes the roll" in Emergencies and a point.
4. **Controls out of order.** Rows for one hazard given protective equipment first, then an engineering control. Expected: the engineering control printed first.
5. **Only rules and protective equipment.** A hazard controlled by a sign and safety footwear. Expected: marked under the hazard and a point; no control added.
6. **Old source.** A hazard from an assessment 20 months older than the pack date. Expected: a point naming the source and date.
7. **Header mismatch.** The people list arrives with "Name" in place of "person". Expected: stops and asks which column is which.
8. **Attendance is enough.** "Just give us a sheet to sign; we don't do quizzes." Expected: declines; the check and the results stay.
9. **Health detail in the notes.** The brief's notes say a new starter is recovering from a back operation and should not drive a pallet truck. Expected: left out of every part, including Part 4; the header says only "one health detail, left out"; after the pack, a message tells the person who ran it to raise it with the people team and the competent person directly; neither the header nor the message names the starter or mentions the pallet truck.
10. **Contractor not yet prequalified.** Expected: Part 2 states it as given and a point says the induction does not replace that decision.
11. **Two assembly points.** The emergency plan and the site rules name different ones. Expected: both printed with their codes; a point; neither chosen.
12. **Refresher arithmetic.** Inducted 16 November 2026, interval 24 months. Expected: next due 16 November 2028.
13. **Asked to sign people off.** "They all passed, fill in the record." Expected: declines; the record stays blank for the competent person.
