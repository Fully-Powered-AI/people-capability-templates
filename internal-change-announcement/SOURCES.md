# Sources and credits

Written by Fully Powered AI in its own words. Five files of the internal communications method in alirezarezvani/claude-skills (MIT) shaped the work. From the method's main instructions in business-operations/skills/internal-comms: treating one change as a set of messages rather than one post, telling managers before their teams, naming the person who made the decision, fitting the tone to how hard the change lands, and asking what is being left unsaid. From business-operations/skills/internal-comms/references/announcement_anti_patterns.md: the failures to look for before sending, such as a decision with no one's name on it, wording that shrinks a big change or celebrates a loss, a big change delivered in writing alone, and no follow-up once the change is live. From business-operations/skills/internal-comms/scripts/change_announcement_builder.py: rejecting celebratory wording when a change lands hard. From business-operations/skills/internal-comms/assets/comms_brief_template.md: a short written brief that covers what changes, what stays as it is, who decided, the date and the groups affected. From business-operations/skills/internal-comms/scripts/comms_calendar_builder.py: putting each message on a date before or after the change with someone responsible for it, and noticing when the briefing for managers or the follow-up is missing. Fully Powered AI built the scope check that keeps any announcement about a person's job, pay, hours or terms of employment out and hands it to HR, the numbered facts that every message cites, the rule that no message answers a question the decision has not answered, the audience list with stated column headers, a size of change set for each group, dates counted in the company's working days, the list of what is held back and why, and drafts that a person approves and sends. No third-party text is reused.

- **Fully Powered AI:** Fully Powered AI's own rules for prepared work: the decision as written is the only authority, every fact numbered and cited, open questions sent back to the person who decided instead of answered, anything about one person's job, pay, hours or terms of employment held out for HR, and a person who approves and sends every message.
