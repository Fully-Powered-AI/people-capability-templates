# Internal change announcement

Workflow template, version 0.1.0.

## Purpose

Prepare the announcement pack for one internal change that has already been decided: the facts in a numbered list, who hears first and on which date, a draft message for each group and each moment, talking points for the people who tell each group, and the questions people will ask with an answer drawn from the facts or marked as needing one. The pack also lists what has been held back and why, and the checks the person who ran it should settle before anything goes out. The assistant prepares; it never sends, posts or schedules a message, never decides the change and never announces anything about one person's job, pay, hours or terms of employment.

## When to use it and when not to

Use it once a change to how people work is decided and before anyone outside the decision has been told: a new system or tool, a new process or approval route, an office or site arrangement, a team structure in which everyone keeps their job, pay and hours, or a workplace policy whose final wording is already approved. Run it again when a fact changes or an open question is answered.

Do not use it:

- for any announcement about one person's employment: a new starter, an appointment, a promotion, a departure, a retirement, a role change, discipline. HR prepares those;
- for any change that removes or creates a position, moves anyone into a different role, or changes anyone's pay, benefits, hours or terms of employment, including redundancies and restructures that remove roles. HR leads those, with the advice it needs;
- to decide whether the change is a good idea, or to settle the details the decision left open. Those go back to the person who decided;
- to write or reword the policy itself. This workflow template announces wording that is already final;
- for messages to customers, suppliers, investors or the press.

## Roles

- **Runs it and approves the wording:** the internal communications lead, the people lead or the operations lead.
- **Decision owner:** the person who made the decision. Approves the facts, answers every open question and leads any meeting for a group rated major.
- **Senders:** the people named on the audience list to tell each group. Each reads and sends their own message.
- **HR contact:** the role that takes anything held back about a person's employment.

## Inputs

1. **The change brief,** typed or pasted. It needs: the change in a sentence; why; what changes; what stays the same; who decided, with their role; the announcement date; the effective date. Useful as well: meetings or training already planned, with dates; who answers questions about what; anything not for this announcement; answers the decision owner has already approved; any draft wording from the decision owner. If the change, who decided, the announcement date or the effective date is missing, stop and ask. If the brief says the decision is not final, stop: this workflow template announces decided changes only.
2. **The audience list,** typed or exported, one row per group, with these default column headers: `audience`, `people`, `what_changes_for_them`, `size_of_change`, `told_by`, `channel`. `size_of_change` is routine, noticeable or major. `told_by` is the name and role of the person who sends that group's message. If the headers do not match these, stop and ask which column holds which before reading any row. If a row has no size, write "size not stated", treat it as major for the sequence, and add a check. If a row has no sender or channel, write "not stated" and add a check.
3. **The company settings:** today's date; the working days of the week and the days the organization is closed; [your manager lead time], [your reminder lead time] and [your check-in delay]; the channel for the manager briefing; who approves the facts and who approves the wording; the HR contact. If some or all are missing, use the defaults in the procedure and say in the header which were defaulted.

Default sizes of change, for the group concerned:

- **Routine:** they need to know, but their own work does not change, or changes in one small step the message can explain.
- **Noticeable:** something they do every week changes, or they take on a new task.
- **Major:** most of their daily work changes, or their reporting line, workplace or the main tool of their role changes.

The assistant works only from what is supplied. It needs no connection to email, chat or any HR system and never asks for a password or a login code.

## The procedure

1. **Check the inputs.** Confirm one change, one brief and one audience list. Check the list's headers against the defaults. Confirm the effective date is on or after the announcement date; if it is earlier, stop and ask.
2. **Check the scope.** If the change itself removes or creates a position, moves anyone into a different role, or changes anyone's pay, benefits, hours or terms of employment, or announces an appointment, promotion, departure, retirement or disciplinary matter, decline the whole pack and say the HR contact leads it. If the change is in scope but one item in the brief is of that kind, hold that item out: it goes under "Held out of every message" without repeating the person's name or the detail, and no message mentions it. If the brief does not say whether anyone's job, pay or hours change, write so on the Scope line and add the question under "Questions people will ask" as needing an answer.
3. **List the facts.** Number every fact in the brief as F1, F2 and on, one fact each, in plain words, adding nothing. From the audience list, a group's `what_changes_for_them` is a fact where it says something the brief does not. Headcounts, senders and channels are not numbered as facts: they set the sequence, the message headers and who is named. An answer the decision owner has already approved is a fact. An item held out in step 2, or marked not for this announcement, is not listed. Wording that only restates the sequence, such as "this morning", "tomorrow, Friday 4 December" or "before your teams hear", is allowed in a message and needs no fact number.
4. **Build the sequence.** Count dates in working days, skipping non-working weekdays and closed days. Defaults:
   - **Manager briefing:** [your manager lead time] working days before the announcement date (default 2), from the decision owner to every sender on the audience list other than the decision owner, by email unless the settings say otherwise. If that date is before today, keep it and add a check.
   - **Announcement day:** first, any meeting the brief names for a group rated major; then the written messages, most affected group first (major, then noticeable, then routine), with the widest group last.
   - **Events from the brief,** such as training, on their own dates.
   - **Reminder:** [your reminder lead time] working days before the effective date (default 2), to each group rated noticeable or major, from that group's sender.
   - **The effective date.**
   - **Check-in:** [your check-in delay] working days after the effective date (default 10), to each group rated noticeable or major, from the decision owner.
   If a group rated major has no meeting in the brief where people can ask questions, write the LIVE SESSION MISSING line and add a check. Never invent a meeting.
5. **Draft the messages.** One message for each written entry in the sequence, from the sender and on the channel the list gives, except where step 4 names the sender (the manager briefing and the check-in come from the decision owner). In this order, leaving out what does not apply: what is changing and when; who decided, in the first person when the sender is the decision owner; why, in the brief's terms; what it means for this group; what stays the same; what they need to do and by when; the open questions; who to ask about what. Use short sentences in plain words, active voice, and no abbreviation the brief does not spell out. Use only the listed facts. Add no benefit, result, reassurance, date, invitation or promise the facts do not state. A promise to pass on an answer is allowed; a promised outcome is not. A message never tells its readers on its own authority that a point is not settled, undecided or still open. A question marked ANSWER NEEDED in step 7 bears on a group when the question lists that group under "asked by" or that group's sender's talking points name it. Each message to such a group on the announcement day carries a bracketed note for the decision owner instead: "[Before sending, [decision owner] confirms Q[n] is still open. If it is: '[the sentence saying it is not settled and that the answer will be passed on]'. If it has been decided, put the answer here.]" The manager briefing carries the same note for every such question that bears on a sender's group. A reminder to such a group carries a bracketed note to add the answer once it is decided: "[Add the answer to Q[n], on [the point], once [decision owner] has decided it.]" A check-in carries a bracketed note for the person to add what has happened since the effective date; it reports no result itself. Name no individual other than the people the brief gives a part in the change. After each message, list the facts it uses.
6. **Write the talking points.** One set for each meeting in the sequence, for the person who leads it, and one for each sender other than the decision owner, for the questions their group will bring them: what changes and when; who decided; why; what stays the same; what people need to do; and what to say when asked something the facts do not answer: that the question goes to the decision owner and the answer will be passed on, naming the ANSWER NEEDED questions by number. A sender other than the decision owner says a point is not settled only once the decision owner has confirmed it is open. Talking points use only the listed facts.
7. **List the questions people will ask.** For each group, the questions its people would reasonably ask given what changes for them. Always consider: why, and why now; who decided; what do I have to do, and by when; what happens to work already under way; what stays the same; does this change my job, pay or hours; who do I ask. Add a question for anything that stops or is taken away. Answer each from the facts only, citing them. Where the facts do not answer it, write ANSWER NEEDED from the decision owner, say what has to be decided, and say what the messages and talking points say until then. Mark ANSWER NEEDED only in these cases: the facts show something stopping, moving or changing and leave a consequence of it unstated (the old spreadsheet stops, but nothing says what happens to orders still on it), citing the facts that show it; the brief itself names a point as open; the brief does not say whether anyone's job, pay or hours change (step 2); or you were asked to add a reassurance the brief does not state. A question the facts give no sign of, such as why a particular date was chosen or how a team covers absences, is not raised: the decision owner can add it. Answer the question about job, pay or hours only in the words the brief uses.
8. **List what is held back.** Each held-out item with its reason: about one person's employment (HR announces it separately), not for this announcement as the brief says, or not yet decided. Say what managers should do if asked: say nothing beyond the announcement and pass the question to the HR contact or the decision owner.
9. **Run the checks.** Read the brief's draft wording and your own drafts against these lists. Fix your own drafts; report what remains for the person who ran it.
   - **Decision with no name:** "it has been decided", "the decision was taken", "it was agreed", "the business has decided", with no person named.
   - **Shrinking a change:** "just", "small tweak", "quick change", "nothing much", "only a", for a group rated noticeable or major.
   - **Celebrating a loss:** "exciting", "delighted", "great news", "proud to announce", "can't wait", where something stops or is taken away for that group.
   - **Size may be low:** the facts meet a higher size than the list gives, by the definitions in the inputs. State the size the facts meet; never change the size yourself. Leave the sequence as the list's size sets it, and say which reminder and check-in, with their dates and senders, that group would also get if the person rates it higher.
   - **Date near a likely closure:** a reminder, check-in or event from the brief falls in a period when many organizations close or many people are away, such as the days around a public holiday or the end of the year, and the settings list no closed day there. State the date; the person confirms it or moves it.
   - **Live session missing**, **briefing date passed**, **size not stated**, **sender or channel not stated.**
10. **Return the pack** to the person who ran it. The decision owner approves the facts, the person who ran it approves the wording, and each sender sends their own message.

## Exceptions and stop conditions

- **No brief or no audience list:** stop and ask.
- **The brief lacks the change, who decided, the announcement date or the effective date:** stop and ask.
- **Audience list headers do not match the defaults:** stop and ask which column holds which.
- **The effective date is before the announcement date:** stop and ask.
- **The brief says the decision is not final:** stop; announce decided changes only.
- **The change removes or creates a position, moves anyone into a different role, or changes anyone's pay, benefits, hours or terms of employment, or is about one person's employment:** decline the pack and name the HR contact. One such item inside an otherwise ordinary change is held out, without the name or the detail.
- **Asked to add a reassurance or promise the brief does not state,** such as "nobody will be affected": decline, and add the question as ANSWER NEEDED from the decision owner.
- **Asked to decide the change, fill in a detail the decision left open, or write the policy itself:** decline and name the decision owner.
- **Asked to send, post or schedule a message:** decline; each sender sends their own.
- **The brief names a member of staff in connection with health, performance, discipline or a complaint:** do not repeat the name or the detail anywhere in the pack.
- **The inputs contain a password, a login code or bank details:** do not repeat them, and tell the person to remove them from the source.

## Reviewer checklist

1. Every fact in the list is in the brief or the audience list, and nothing marked not for announcement is listed. (Decision owner)
2. Every message and talking point uses only listed facts, and its "Facts used" line is right. (Person who ran it)
3. No message promises an outcome, a result or a reassurance the facts do not state. (Decision owner)
4. Managers are briefed before their teams, a group rated major hears in a meeting before it reads anything, and the dates match the working-day calendar. (Person who ran it)
5. Every question is either answered from the facts or marked ANSWER NEEDED, every ANSWER NEEDED rests on a change the facts show, a point the brief names as open or the job, pay or hours rule, and every open question is listed for the decision owner. No message tells its readers a point is unsettled unless the decision owner has confirmed it. (Decision owner)
6. Nothing about one person's job, pay, hours or terms of employment appears in any message, and every held-out item gives no name or detail. (HR contact)
7. The decision owner is named as the person who decided, and the wording neither shrinks the change nor celebrates a loss. (Person who ran it)
8. Each sender has read their own message and talking points. (Each sender)

## Acceptance tests

Proposed tests. None has been run.

1. **Ordinary change.** A new expense app for all staff, rated noticeable, with a decision owner, both dates and a training session. Expected: facts listed, briefing dated two working days before the announcement, messages drafted, reminder and check-in dated, no checks beyond those the inputs raise.
2. **No decision owner.** The brief leaves "Decided by" blank. Expected: stops and asks; drafts nothing.
3. **Wrong headers.** The list has `Group, Headcount, Impact, Owner, Medium`. Expected: stops and asks which column holds which.
4. **A line about someone leaving.** The brief adds that a named manager is retiring. Expected: held out with no name or detail, reason "about one person's employment", and no message mentions it.
5. **Roles removed.** The change merges two teams and ends three roles. Expected: declines the whole pack and names the HR contact.
6. **Unanswered question.** The brief says the old system stops but not what happens to work already in it. Expected: ANSWER NEEDED from the decision owner, citing the fact that the old system stops; each message to that group carries a bracketed note for the decision owner to confirm the point is open or add the answer; a bracketed note in the reminder.
7. **Size looks low.** A group rated routine takes on a new weekly task. Expected: the size is left as given; a check states the facts meet noticeable.
8. **Major group with no meeting.** Expected: the LIVE SESSION MISSING line and a check; no meeting invented.
9. **Passive, shrinking draft wording.** The decision owner's draft says "It has been decided ... just a small tweak" for a group rated major. Expected: not used; both flagged, in one check or two; the drafts name the decision owner.
10. **Asked to add "nobody will be affected".** The brief does not say so. Expected: declines; the question is listed as ANSWER NEEDED.
11. **Asked to send.** Expected: declines; each sender sends their own message.
12. **Closed day in the count.** The office is closed on a day inside the manager lead time. Expected: the briefing moves a working day earlier.
13. **A question nobody has a reason to ask.** The brief gives an effective date and no reason for choosing it, and nothing in it is shown stopping or changing on that date beyond the change itself. Expected: no ANSWER NEEDED about why that date, and no message says the reason for the date is not settled.
14. **Check-in at the year end.** The check-in falls in the last week of December and the settings list no closed days then. Expected: the date is kept and a check asks the person to confirm or move it.

## What to customize

- [your manager lead time]: how many working days before the announcement managers are briefed. Suggested default: 2.
- [your reminder lead time] and [your check-in delay]: when the reminder and the check-in go out. Suggested defaults: 2 working days before the effective date, and 10 working days after it.
- What counts as routine, noticeable and major for your people. The defaults are in the inputs.
- The words the drafts avoid. Add your own, such as the phrases your leaders use that staff have learned to distrust.
- The column headers of your audience list, if your planning sheet uses others.
- The channels you use, and who sends the manager briefing if not the decision owner.
- Who approves the facts, who approves the wording, and the HR contact for held-out items.
- Which documents the assistant may read in ChatGPT, Claude, Copilot or Gemini. It needs no access to email, chat or any HR system.
