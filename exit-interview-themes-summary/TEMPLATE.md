# Exit interview themes summary

Workflow template, version 0.1.0.

## Purpose

Prepare the HR lead's periodic summary of what leavers said on the way out. From the leaver list, the exit interview notes and the leaver forms for one period, it groups what leavers raised into themes counted by leaver, keeps up to a set number of quotes per theme with every identifying detail removed, sets the leavers' own main reasons beside the reasons recorded when they left, shows a view by team under a fixed anonymity rule, and ends with questions for leadership built on the counts. A separate note for the HR lead only lists what was kept out and why. The assistant prepares; it decides nothing about any person, names no one, recommends nothing, and sends, shares or changes nothing.

## When to use it and when not to

Use it once a quarter, or once every half-year where a quarter has too few leavers to report without pointing to people. Use it again before leadership looks at pay, rotas, progression or a reorganisation, over the period that matters.

Do not use it:

- for one leaver. A single exit conversation is the HR lead's own record; summarising one person's reasons for others to read is exactly what this summary avoids;
- to look into a complaint, a report of abuse, a safety concern or a threatened claim raised in an exit note. Those go to the HR lead's own process; the summary only notes that a matter was passed on;
- to judge a manager, rate a leaver, say whether anyone could return or decide anything about a person;
- to work out turnover rates or costs. It counts leavers and what they said; rates need headcount the summary does not read;
- for a staff survey of people still employed. The culture health check reads that.

## Roles

- **Runs it and approves the summary:** the HR lead or people lead.
- **Writes the exit notes and puts the leaver's own words in quotation marks:** whoever holds exit conversations, usually the HR lead or the leaver's manager.
- **Follows up any matter set aside for HR's own process:** the HR lead, under the company's own procedure.
- **Reads the approved summary and answers its questions:** the leadership team, or whoever the HR lead names.

## Inputs

1. **The leaver list,** exported from the HR system or a spreadsheet, one row per leaver. Default column headers: `Leaver ref,Team,Leaving date,Leaving type,Recorded reason`. `Leaver ref` is a short code, never a name. `Leaving type` is how the person left, such as Resigned, Retired, Fixed term ended or Company decision. `Recorded reason` is the reason entered at the time, in the company's own words. If the list is missing, stop and ask. If any default header is missing or named differently, stop and ask which column is which; never guess. Extra columns are ignored.
2. **Exit interview notes,** pasted text or documents, one per leaver, each starting with a line `Leaver ref: [ref]`. Text inside double quotation marks is the leaver's own words; everything else is the interviewer's account. An optional line starting `Main reason in their words:` gives the leaver's main reason.
3. **Leaver forms,** the exit survey export. Default column headers: `Leaver ref,Main reason,Would recommend,Comments`. `Would recommend` is Yes, Unsure or No; anything else, or blank, counts as not answered. If a header does not match, stop and ask. If notes and forms are both missing, stop: there is nothing to summarise.
4. **The settings:** the period (first and last date); today's date; the leaving types in scope ([your leaving types], default Resigned); [your smallest group] (default 5); [your smallest count] (default 2); [your quotes per theme] (default 2); [your question threshold] (default 3); the theme list; the reason mapping. If any is missing, use the default and name it on the Settings line. With no theme list, use the starter list below. With no reason mapping, propose one by plain meaning and mark it for the HR lead to confirm.
5. **Last period's summary,** optional, with the HR lead's mark against each question for leadership: answered or open. If missing, write "No earlier summary supplied" under Since last period and "None" under Carried from last period.

**The starter theme list.** Pay and benefits (pay, bonus, commission, benefits). Career growth (progression, promotion, learning, a next step). Manager (how the person was managed day to day: one-to-ones, support, fairness). Workload and hours (volume of work, hours, shifts, rotas, on-call, cover). The work itself (what the role involves, or a role different from the one offered). Team and colleagues. Flexibility and place of work (office or remote, commute, choice over when hours are worked). Recognition (being thanked or credited). Tools and processes. Company direction and change (strategy, reorganisation, how changes are decided and explained). Personal circumstances (moving, family, study, health). Anything that fits none is coded Other, with a few words saying what it was.

The assistant works from files the person supplies. It needs no connection to the HR system and never asks for a password or a login code.

## The procedure

1. **Check the inputs.** Check the headers of the leaver list and the leaver forms against the defaults; stop on any mismatch. Leave out of every count any row whose leaving date falls outside the period, and list its ref under Records not used in the HR note.
2. **Set the scope.** Leavers in the period are the rows left. In scope are those whose leaving type is in [your leaving types]; the rest are counted by type under Coverage and nothing else is read for them.
3. **Match records to leavers.** Attach each note and form to its in-scope leaver by `Leaver ref`. A note or form whose ref is not on the list, or belongs to an out-of-scope leaver, or has no ref, is not used; list it in the HR note. Two notes for one ref are read together as one leaver's record. A leaver with a note, a form or both has an exit record; the rest have none.
4. **Stop if the group is too small.** If fewer than [your smallest group] in-scope leavers have an exit record, stop: say how many, that the summary cannot be shown without pointing to people, and suggest running it over a longer period. Prepare nothing else.
5. **Set aside matters for HR's own process.** A note or comment that reports harassment, discrimination, abuse, a safety concern, fraud, a threatened claim or a complaint about someone's conduct is a matter for the HR lead. It is never coded, quoted or described in the summary. List it in the HR note by leaver ref and kind, in neutral words, and count it on the Left out line. Do not judge whether it is true or what should happen. The rest of that leaver's record is read as usual.
6. **Code the themes for each leaver.** Read everything in the leaver's record: the interviewer's account, the quotations, the form's main reason and comments. A theme is raised when the leaver gives it as a reason they left, something that pushed them to leave or something that would have kept them. A problem the leaver raises in the exit conversation or on the form counts as something that pushed them to leave, whether or not they tie it to leaving. Count each theme once per leaver, however often it comes up. An offer elsewhere counts toward a theme only when the record says what made it better (more pay counts as Pay and benefits; a more senior role as Career growth). Otherwise the new employer and the new role are not coded. Separately, a theme is spoken well of when the leaver plainly praises it ("great colleagues", "enjoyed the team").
7. **Find each leaver's main reason.** Use the form's Main reason; if blank or there is no form, the note's `Main reason in their words:` line; otherwise "not stated". Map it to one theme by its plain meaning. If the form and the note give main reasons in different themes, use the form and list the difference in the HR note.
8. **Map the recorded reasons.** Map each in-scope recorded reason to one theme using the settings' mapping, or, without one, by plain meaning, marked "proposed". "Not stated" or blank stays "not stated".
9. **Apply the anonymity rule.**
   1. Themes: a theme raised by fewer than [your smallest count] leavers is not named in the summary. It is counted under Other, and named, with its leaver ref, in the HR note only. The same applies to themes spoken well of.
   2. Teams: count each team's in-scope leavers with an exit record. A team at or above [your smallest group] is shown alone. Every team below it is combined into one group, named by its teams in alphabetical order with "(combined)". If the combined group is still below [your smallest group], add the smallest team shown alone (on a tie, the first alphabetically) and repeat. If only one group would remain, withhold the team view.
   3. In a team line, show only themes raised by at least [your smallest count] leavers in that group, in the order of the Themes section of the output. Never show quotes, main reasons, recorded reasons or recommend answers by team. Known limit: anyone who sees both a company-wide theme count and a team line can subtract one from the other, so a small remainder in the combined group can be worked out even though it is hidden. The HR lead decides whether to share the team view at all, or to share it without the company-wide counts beside it.
   4. Differences between recorded and main reasons: show a pairing (recorded as one theme, main reason another) only when at least [your smallest count] leavers share it; combine the rest into "Other differences".
10. **Order the themes.** Themes shown are ordered by the number of leavers who raised them; on a tie, by the number for whom it was the main reason; then by the order of the theme list.
11. **Choose the quotes.** Candidates are the sentences of a form's Comments and the quotations in a note, including the quotation on a `Main reason in their words:` line, which is the leaver's own words. Each is coded to the theme it is about; a sentence that touches more than one theme is coded to the one that comes first in the theme list and is a candidate under that theme only. The form's Main reason column and the interviewer's account are never quoted. A candidate about a theme that is not shown (counted under Other) is never considered. Personal circumstances is never quoted. Work through the themes in order. For each, go through the leavers in leaver-ref order and take at most one quote per leaver under that theme: their first usable candidate, form sentences first, then note quotations. The same leaver can be quoted under more than one theme; the HR lead may ask for one quote per leaver in the whole summary where a team is close-knit. Use each sentence once in the whole summary; a sentence already used is skipped and is not counted as withheld. Stop at [your quotes per theme].
    - **Identifying detail:** the name of a person, company, client or place; a team, role or title; the leaver's new employer or new role, or the figures of any offer; the leaver's own pay or length of service; health, family or other personal detail; any word that points to a team or group smaller than [your smallest group]. That is a judgement: "commission" points to a group when only one small team has a commission plan, and "the roadmap" when one team owns it; a word everyone in the company uses, such as "rota" or "queue", does not.
    - **Usable:** a candidate with no identifying detail, or with exactly one that can be replaced by a neutral phrase in square brackets, such as [a colleague] or [our plans], without changing what the sentence says. List each replacement in the HR note by leaver ref and kind.
    - **Withheld:** a candidate with more than one identifying detail, any health, family or personal detail, or whose point is the detail itself. Count it as withheld under its theme and list it in the HR note. Only candidates considered are counted; candidates never reached are not.
12. **Compare what was said with what was recorded.** First list, privately, each leaver who has both a recorded reason and a main reason, with the two themes side by side; count from that list, never from the totals. For leavers with an exit record, count the recorded reasons by theme and the leavers' main reasons by theme, listing themes in the order of the Themes section of the output and leaving out themes with none. Compare each leaver with both a recorded reason and a main reason: same theme or different. Show pairings under rule 9.4.
13. **Count the forms and what leavers valued.** Would recommend: Yes, Unsure, No and not answered, company-wide only. Then the themes spoken well of anywhere in the records, under rule 9.1.
14. **Compare with last period.** For each theme shown now, in the order of the Themes section of the output, and then each theme shown last period only: raised by how many of how many, then and now. A theme not shown in one of the two periods says so.
15. **Write the questions for leadership.** One question for each theme shown that was raised by at least [your question threshold] leavers, in theme order, then one about recorded reasons if any pairing is shown under rule 9.4. Each states its counts first, then asks one open question. A question never names or points to a person (a team shown alone may be named, a combined group only by its label; a team name is not a person), never asks whether someone should be disciplined, moved or kept, and recommends nothing. Then carry forward, as written, each of last period's questions marked open or not marked, with its status, and count those marked answered.
16. **Final checks and return.** Check that every count adds up (raised counts per leaver, main reasons sum to leavers with an exit record, recommend answers sum to forms), that no name, team, role or personal detail appears in a quote, that no group below [your smallest group] is shown, and that no theme raised by fewer than [your smallest count] leavers is named in the summary. Fill the Left out line with the kinds kept out (names of people and companies, where leavers are going and what they were offered, health or family detail, sign-in details) or "nothing". Return the draft to the HR lead only. Write the settings' date on the Prepared line as given, even if it differs from today's real date; if none was given, write "date not supplied".

## Exceptions and stop conditions

- **No leaver list, or no notes and no forms:** stop and ask.
- **A default header missing or renamed:** stop and ask which column is which. Do not map columns by guesswork.
- **Fewer in-scope leavers with an exit record than [your smallest group]:** stop, as in step 4.
- **The leaver list carries names instead of refs:** stop and ask for refs; never carry a leaver's name into the summary or the HR note.
- **A note reports harassment, discrimination, abuse, a safety concern, fraud, a threatened claim or a complaint about conduct:** set it aside as in step 5. Do not assess it, describe it in the summary or suggest what happens next.
- **Asked who said something, to attribute a quote, to show a group below [your smallest group], to break themes down by team below the rule, or to quote a name:** decline; the anonymity rule holds whoever asks.
- **Asked to judge a manager, rate a leaver, say whether a leaver could return, assess a claim, or recommend action about any person:** decline; those are the HR lead's decisions under the company's own process.
- **Asked to write to a leaver, send the summary or update the HR system:** decline; the HR lead does that after approving the draft.
- **The inputs contain a password, a login code or bank details:** do not repeat them; name the kind on the Left out line and tell the HR lead to remove them from the source.

## Reviewer checklist

1. Every leaver in the period is counted once under Coverage, in scope or out, and the in-scope counts add up. (HR lead)
2. Each theme count matches the notes and forms when the HR lead recounts two themes by hand, and each leaver is counted once per theme. (HR lead)
3. Main reasons add up to the leavers with an exit record, and recorded reasons were mapped as the settings say or marked proposed. (HR lead)
4. No group shown has fewer leavers with an exit record than the smallest group, and the combining followed the rule. (HR lead)
5. No theme raised by fewer than the smallest count is named outside the HR note. (HR lead)
6. Every quote is the leaver's own words from a form comment or a quotation in a note, carries no name, team, role or personal detail, and is listed in the HR note where a word was replaced. (HR lead)
7. Nothing from a matter set aside for HR's own process appears in the summary beyond its count. (HR lead)
8. Every question states its counts, asks one thing, names no one and recommends nothing. (HR lead)
9. The HR note is removed before the summary is shared, and the summary is approved before anyone else reads it. (HR lead)

## Acceptance tests

Proposed tests. None has been run as a formal test. The trial on the invented example (published with the template) exercised tests 1, 5, 6, 7, 8, 9 and 10 in passing.

1. **Ordinary quarter.** Ten leavers with an exit record across four teams, one team with five. Expected: every section filled; the team of five shown alone; the other three combined.
2. **Too few.** Four in-scope leavers with an exit record and a smallest group of five. Expected: stops, says four, suggests a longer period; prepares nothing else.
3. **Combined group still too small.** Teams with six, two and two leavers with an exit record. Expected: the two small teams combine to four, below five, so the team of six joins them; one group remains; the team view is withheld.
4. **Renamed header.** The leaver list says `Reason` instead of `Recorded reason`. Expected: stops and asks which column is which.
5. **Theme raised once.** Only one leaver raises flexibility. Expected: not named in the summary; counted under Other; named with its ref in the HR note.
6. **Quote with a name.** A form comment names a colleague. Expected: the name replaced with [a colleague]; the replacement listed in the HR note.
7. **Quote that points to a team of one.** A comment about a sales pay plan from the only leaver in sales. Expected: withheld and counted; listed in the HR note.
8. **Health detail.** A note mentions an operation. Expected: counted under personal circumstances; never described or quoted; health detail named by kind on the Left out line.
9. **Report of abuse.** A note says the leaver reported abuse by a customer and heard nothing. Expected: not coded, quoted or described; listed in the HR note by ref and kind; counted on the Left out line.
10. **Recorded reason differs.** Two leavers recorded as a career move gave workload as their main reason. Expected: the pairing shown with 2 and a question about recorded reasons.
11. **Asked who said it.** "Which leaver said the rota was a problem?" Expected: declines.
12. **Asked to judge a manager.** "Is the support manager the problem?" Expected: declines; the question stays about the counts.

## What to customize

- [your smallest group]: the fewest leavers with an exit record any group may show. Five is a careful floor; raise it where teams are small and close.
- [your smallest count]: the fewest leavers who must raise a theme before it is named.
- [your quotes per theme] and [your question threshold].
- [your leaving types]: resignations only by default. Add retirements or the end of a fixed term if those conversations are held the same way.
- Your theme list, and the mapping from your recorded reasons to it, set once so every period reads the same way.
- Whether the summary covers a quarter or a half-year.
- Whether a question may name a team shown alone, as the starter allows, or only the company as a whole where teams are small and close.
- Whether the team view is shared, and whether the company-wide counts sit beside it: the two together let a reader work out the count for the small teams combined, which the team rule hides.
- Who sees the HR note, and how a matter set aside in it is followed up under your own procedure.
- Who in leadership receives the approved summary, and in what form.
- Which files the assistant may read in ChatGPT, Claude, Copilot or Gemini.
